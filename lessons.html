Our approach

The Outlining Method

The reason beginners play a real song in their first month — not their first year.

Most beginner methods make students drill scales and isolated techniques for months before they touch a real song. The result: kids get bored and quit before it ever sounds like music.

The Outlining Method flips that. We start by playing an outline of the song — the essential chord shapes, simplified so a complete beginner can play them on day one. The student is jamming along to the actual recording almost immediately. Momentum builds fast when it sounds right from the start. As the shapes become natural, we layer the full technique back in step by step.

Strumming songs (e.g., Zombie)

  1. Chord shapes first. Learn to fret the three chords — no strumming pattern yet. Just hold each shape cleanly.
  2. Outline along to the song. Switch between chord shapes on time while the recording plays. It already sounds like the song.
  3. Add rhythm gradually. Once chord changes feel natural, introduce the strumming pattern one beat at a time until the full version is there.

Fingerpicking songs (e.g., Dust in the Wind)

  1. Simplified outline first. Break the fingerpicking pattern down to its bare skeleton — just the root note and the top string, played together.
  2. Play along right away. Even the stripped-down version fits the song. The student sounds musical from lesson one.
  3. Layer the full pattern back in. Add one finger at a time until the complete fingerpicking arrangement is in place.

Skill levels

Where does your child fit?

Every level starts with the question: what do you want to play?

Beginner

Never touched a guitar

We start from zero — holding the guitar, fretting the first chord, and playing along to a real song within the first few lessons. No experience needed at all.

  • Chord shapes & clean fretting
  • Basic rhythm and strumming
  • First full song in ~30 days
  • Building muscle memory painlessly
Intermediate

Know a few chords, ready for more

Already past the basics but stuck on a plateau? We break through it with harder songs, barre chords, more complex strumming, and an expanded song repertoire.

  • Barre chords & full chord library
  • Fingerpicking patterns
  • Scales for lead playing
  • Song arrangement & dynamics
Advanced

Solid player, chasing mastery

Advanced students work on technique refinement, theory they can actually use, improvisation, and tackling the songs that felt impossible before.

  • Music theory applied to the fretboard
  • Improvisation & soloing
  • Classical or advanced fingerstyle
  • Difficult repertoire & performance prep
